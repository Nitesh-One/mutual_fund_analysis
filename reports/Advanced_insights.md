# Advanced Insights

## 1. Downside Risk Analysis (VaR & CVaR)

The Value at Risk (VaR 95%) and Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR 95%) analysis revealed that Small Cap and Mid Cap funds exhibited the highest downside risk among all schemes. These funds experienced the largest expected losses during adverse market conditions, while Large Cap and Gilt funds demonstrated comparatively lower risk exposure. CVaR values were consistently more negative than VaR, indicating that losses during extreme downside events can be substantially larger than the average expected risk.

## 2. Investor Cohort Behavior

Investor Cohort Analysis showed that recent cohorts contributed the highest total investment amounts and maintained larger average SIP values compared to earlier cohorts. Investors who entered the market during later years demonstrated stronger participation levels, reflecting growing awareness and adoption of mutual fund investing. Large Cap and Flexi Cap funds emerged as the most preferred investment choices across multiple cohorts.

## 3. SIP Continuity and Retention

SIP Continuity Analysis identified the majority of investors as active and maintaining regular investment schedules. However, a subset of investors exhibited average gaps exceeding 35 days between SIP transactions and were classified as At-Risk. These investors may require targeted engagement strategies to improve retention and reduce SIP discontinuation rates.

## 4. Risk-Adjusted Performance Leadership

Sharpe Ratio analysis highlighted a group of funds that consistently delivered superior risk-adjusted returns. Several Large Cap and Flexi Cap funds achieved higher Sharpe Ratios, indicating efficient conversion of portfolio risk into returns. Funds with strong Sharpe Ratios generally also ranked highly in the composite Fund Score, validating their overall performance quality.

## 5. Benchmark Relative Performance

Benchmark comparison against NIFTY 50 and NIFTY 100 demonstrated that a select group of funds consistently outperformed their benchmark indices over the three-year evaluation period. Positive Alpha values indicated that these funds generated excess returns beyond what could be explained by market exposure alone. Funds with lower tracking error exhibited more stable benchmark alignment, while higher tracking error funds reflected more active management strategies.
